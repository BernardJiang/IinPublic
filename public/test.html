Become an IinPublic tester

A closed test on Google Play, before the public launch — takes about 5 minutes to join.

Build your digital you, one answer at a time. Talk to hundreds of people about hundreds of topics, simultaneously — write a question once (a "Talk"), and your chatbot repeats your approved answer for you, every time it comes up again. It never invents an answer for you, only reuses one you already gave. It's peer-to-peer: your identity and conversations live on your own device, not in a company database.

1. Join the test

Tap the button below, signed into the Google account you want to test with. Google requires an explicit opt-in click — being on this page isn't enough.

Become a tester on Google Play

Click "Become a tester," then "Download it on Google Play." Install and open the app like any other Play Store app.
Stay opted in for the whole test. Google requires testers to stay continuously opted in — if you opt out early, even by uninstalling and it triggering an opt-out, your days don't carry over. If you're not going to have time to actually use it, it's more useful for us if you opt out right away rather than partway through.

2. What to actually do in the app

No login, no account setup — the app generates your identity on first launch. A short guided tour walks you through writing your first real Talk (an actual editable draft, not a demo).

  • Write at least one real Talk — a genuine question you'd actually want an answer to.
  • Answer a few Talks from other people in your regional chat room.
  • If you know another tester, try to actually match with them — join the same room, answer each other's Talks, and see a real conversation form. This is the part that's hardest to test alone.
  • Poke around Settings — language, appearance, and the "Download the app" section (which will correctly say Android is "Coming soon" there for now — that's expected, not a bug).

3. Send feedback

The fastest way: open Contacts → TechSupport right inside the app and just ask or say what you found — that channel goes straight to us. For anything with a screenshot or more detail worth writing up, use GitHub Issues instead (the project is open source).

Useful things to mention: anything confusing in the first-run guide, anything that crashed or froze, whether matching actually found you real people, and anything you expected the app to do that it didn't.

Privacy

See the full privacy policy — short version: no email/password signup, your talks and messages travel device-to-device, and precise location is never shared (only a coarse ~2km region). The one exception is TechSupport, which is answered by us directly, so don't send anything there you wouldn't want us to read.
